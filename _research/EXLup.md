Long ago, the observations of young stellar objects (YSOs) that is still undergoing active disk accretion appear underluminous compared to model predictions, which then believes the mass accretion is a steady process [(Kenyon et al. 1990)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1990AJ.....99..869K/abstract). 

In light of this, the episodic accretion paradigm is proposed - the YSOs should spend most of their time in low-accretion state, but undergo episodic accretion epoch when their accretion rate is raised orders of magnitude higher ([Hartmann 1998](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1998CAS....32.....H/abstract), Figure 1).

Truely, there exists a unique group known as eruptive young stars, which are typically divided into two main categories: EXors and FUors. More comprehensive introduction is covered in [this PPVII paper](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ASPC..534..355F/abstract). In our research, we focus on EX Lup, which is the archetype for the EXor class—a group of young stars known for undergoing repeated, months-long accretion outbursts at optical wavelengths. 

Despite extensive monitoring of EX Lup dating back 130 years, its accretion history remained mostly qualitative with large uncertainties (Figure 2). To give a more quantitative and systematic evaluation, we assess historical accretion rates of EX Lup by applying correlations between optical brightness and accretion, using data from a recent $$\sim2$$ mag optical burst in 2022.

Through our analysis, we found two distinct classes of bursts with different underlying driving causes (Figure 3):
*   **Major Outbursts:** We found that these massive eruptions ($$\Delta V\sim5$$ mag) are rare and have year-long durations. At their peak, they reach accretion rates of roughly $$10^{-7}$$ solar masses per year, and we calculated a total accreted mass of around 0.1 Earth masses per outburst.
*   **Characteristic Bursts:** We identified that these smaller bursts ($$\Delta V\sim2$$ mag) are much more common but only last for about 2 to 3 months. They reach peak accretion rates of roughly $$10^{-8}$$ solar masses per year, accumulating a total mass of around $$10^{-3}$$ Earth masses per burst.

Crucially, we found that the total mass accreted during these two modes is around two times the mass accreted during periods of quiescence. This demonstrates that the star accretes comparable amounts of mass across its active and quiet states, a discovery that provides important observational constraints on the final stages of stellar mass assembly.
